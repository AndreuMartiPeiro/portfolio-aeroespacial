Objective and scope

This section reproduces the thermodynamic cycle of the Williams F107-WR-402 turbofan, the engine selected to power the missile, and evaluates how thrust and fuel consumption respond to its two most influential design parameters.

Published engine data are complemented with justified estimates for the parameters that are not public. With this model the cruise design point is calculated and a map of 49 × 43 combinations of pressure ratio and temperature is evaluated.

Design point: flow path

At the cruise condition the model delivers 2.90 kN of net thrust with a specific fuel consumption of 22.83 g/(kN·s). Figure 1 follows the core flow: compression raises the total pressure to 1608 kPa and the combustor heats the gas to 1227 K; the turbines extract the energy that drives the HP compressor and the fan, and in the mixer the hot flow joins the bypass stream before expanding through the nozzle.

Reading the result

  • The nozzle is choked: the jet leaves under-expanded, which is usual for convergent nozzles.
  • With BPR = 1 the jet is fast and the propulsive efficiency (48.6 %) is moderate: the price of a compact engine that fits inside the fuselage.
  • The HP turbine runs uncooled, which caps the TET at 1227 K in exchange for a simpler engine.

Reading the parametric study

1

Temperature drives thrust

At PR = 6, raising the TET from 1104 to 1500 K doubles the thrust (from 2.02 to 4.15 kN). In return, above the optimum temperature consumption rises quickly: at PR = 4 it reaches 27.0 g/(kN·s) at 1500 K.

2

Each pressure ratio has its optimum temperature

The minimum-consumption TET rises with pressure ratio: 1219 K at PR = 4, 1260 K at PR = 6 and 1333 K at PR = 10. Below it the compressor is oversized; above it the jet leaves too fast.

3

At 1229 K, more compression barely pays off

At the design temperature, going from PR = 6 to PR = 8 only lowers consumption by 2 % (22.85 → 22.36) while thrust drops by 9 % (2.92 → 2.67 kN). At PR = 10 consumption rises again.

4

The design is a practical optimum

The global minimum, 21.41 g/(kN·s), is 6.2 % lower than at design, but it needs 106 K more TET (a cooled turbine) and an HP compressor pressure ratio 65 % higher.

Source Figures read from data/DatosExportados.xlsx (figures 2–4 and the excerpt on page 5).

08

Conclusions and next steps

The model is consistent with the published F107-WR-402 data and with itself: 2.90 kN at 22.83 g/(kN·s) in cruise, and the parametric study reproduces that point within 1 %. The real design gives up 6 % of consumption in exchange for compactness and an uncooled turbine.

Next steps: off-design study (Mach, altitude and power setting) and integration with the aerodynamic analysis, to check that the available thrust matches the missile's cruise drag.
