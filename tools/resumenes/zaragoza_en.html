Objective

Estimate how many passengers Zaragoza Airport will handle in 2050, how they will be spread over the year and what runway infrastructure the busiest hour will require.

The analysis starts from Aena's official monthly figures and links their evolution to the economy, measured by GDP. The result is not a single prediction but a plausible demand range within which the airport must operate.

Method step by step

1 · Cleaning the series

The 24 months of 2020 and 2021 are removed: the pandemic produced anomalous values (4 passengers in April 2020) and disproportionate rebounds in 2021. They are not replaced by estimates; they are simply left out of all calculations, so the core analysis rests on 244 months.

2 · Trend: 12-month centred moving average

MAt = (1/12) · [ 0.5·Xt−6 + Xt−5 + … + Xt+5 + 0.5·Xt+6 ]Xt: passengers in month t. Every calendar month carries the same weight, which cancels out seasonality.

With an even window the centre falls between two months, so the end points carry half weight. Six months are lost at each end: the latest available trend is October 2025 (59,066 passengers a month, ≈ 708,800 a year, consistent with the 707,493 of 2025).

3 · Seasonality: seasonal index

It = Xt / MAt  ·  Im = mean of It over all years for month m  ·  Σ Im ≈ 12

Example: January 2025 had 44,040 passengers against a trend of 58,610.25, so I = 44,040 / 58,610 = 0.75: 25 % below an average month. Averaging every January gives the January index, 0.71.

4 · Elasticity of traffic with respect to GDP

Traffic T and GDP are assumed to follow a power law, T = A · GDPε. Taking logarithms gives a straight line whose slope is the elasticity:

ln T = ln A + ε · ln GDP  →  ε = SLOPE(ln T ; ln GDP)Least squares over 78 quarters (quarterly moving average, 2020–2021 excluded).

Result: ε = 1.07. For every 1 % of GDP growth, traffic grows by 1.07 %. The scatter is wide (R² = 0.11), so GDP explains only part of the variation in traffic.

The isolated point at the bottom right is 2025 Q4, which contains October only. Without it the slope would be 1.50 (note 1, page 6).

5 · Growth scenarios to 2050

The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of the GDP index is used as a reference for the scenarios:

r = (Vfinal / Vinitial)1/t − 1 = (128.15 / 91.99)1/21.5 − 1 = 1.55 % a year86 quarters (21.5 years) between the first and last data points. It supports the 1.5 % base scenario.

6 · From annual traffic to runways

MMT = PAX2050 · Im / 12  →  DMT = MMT / 31  →  HDP = DMT · 0.11  →  mov/h = HDP / (100 · 0.85)  →  runways = mov/h / 40Peak month → average day → peak hour (11 % of the day) → movements (100-seat aircraft at 85 % load) → runways (40 movements per hour, the cautious end of the 40–45 range). Conclusions

One runway is enough in every scenario

The 2050 peak hour requires between 4.6 and 5.3 movements per hour. Even correcting notes 1 to 3 at once (≈ 1.6 M passengers and 67.4 pax/mov) would give about 10 movements per hour, a quarter of the runway's capacity. Own calculation.

After the spreadsheet

In September 2025 Ryanair announced a 45 % capacity cut at Zaragoza, and August 2026 recorded 63,717 passengers (−13.8 % year on year). Since the scenarios start from 2025, they should be reviewed with the 2026 data.
